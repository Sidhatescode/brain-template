# Lantern Club pricing call

Transcript of a 48-minute call between [[ana-reyes]] and [[dev-okonkwo]] on 2026-03-11, nine
days after the Deptford screening. The call where [[lantern-club]] stopped being a ticketed
event business — see [[stop-charging-attendees]].

## What it establishes

Deptford's unit economics, stated as actuals: 41 attendees at £8, £328 revenue, £200 venue,
£60 projector bulb, £68 profit for nine days of work. Ana's objection is not the £68 — it is
the 210 Luma page views against 41 paid tickets, a 20% conversion she attributes entirely to
the price. ⚠️ She concedes on the call that she can't separate price from date; the Hackney
comparison (free, 94 people in a 60-capacity room) is one data point, not a test. The
April experiment was designed to settle it, and did — see [[lantern-club-whatsapp-thread]].

The sponsor route arrives as an existing inbound, not a new idea. Northfield Reels wrote in
February asking what a pre-film logo would cost; Ana didn't reply because she had no number,
and didn't tell Dev. The £400/night quote is invented on this call with no basis given.

## What it reveals about the two of them

Ana reframes a cost problem as a product problem — "the room is the product" — and prices
off audience value rather than off cost recovery. Dev's contribution is the trade nobody
had named:

> "I think we're trading the thing that makes it good for the thing that makes it survive,
> and I want us to at least know that's the trade."

He does not block it. He bounds it: one night, and the logo confined to the pre-roll. That
pattern — concede the direction, constrain the blast radius — recurs. See
[[filtering-versus-filling]].

## The second decision nobody noticed

The nine-days-per-night problem surfaces in the last thirty seconds and gets no argument.
Dev's "a hobby with admin" is the first time either of them treats process as the
constraint. It becomes Priya's 11-step runbook a month later. ⚠️ This is the thread most
likely to be dropped, because neither founder owns it.
