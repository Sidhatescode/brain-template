# The free room fills itself

A small price does not filter an audience down — it collapses it, and the collapse is far
larger than the price would suggest. Across three [[lantern-club]] screenings the door price
was the dominant variable in attendance, and every other explanation (date, venue, film,
promotion) failed to account for the gap.

## The mechanism

A ticket price converts a low-stakes impulse into a decision. Watching an unknown film on a
Tuesday is worth doing on a whim; it is not worth a deliberation. £8 is trivially
affordable to the audience and still fatal, because the cost it imposes is not £8 — it is
the act of choosing. The friction is the checkout, not the number.

Two consequences follow, and both showed up:

**Demand is larger than the priced version suggests.** Deptford converted 41 of 210 page
views at £8. The free April event took 140 signups in four hours and 260 in two weeks
against a 90-seat room. Same organisers, same city, same kind of film, roughly the same
promotion. The priced event was not measuring a small audience — it was measuring the
willingness to transact.

**The sponsor pays more than the audience would.** 41 tickets at £8 is £328 gross and £68
net. One sponsor pays £400 for the same night, net, and renews. The audience is worth more
sold once in aggregate than charged individually, because what a sponsor buys is the room's
composition — people who chose an obscure film on a weeknight — and that is destroyed by
the very filter that monetises it.

## Where it does not apply

The pattern is about events with a high fixed cost and a near-zero marginal cost per
attendee. One more person in a borrowed room costs nothing; the room costs £200 whether four
people come or ninety. That asymmetry is what makes removing the price strictly better once
someone else covers the fixed cost. It would not hold for anything with a real per-unit
cost — catered dinners, printed materials, a paid instructor per head.

It also requires a fixed cost somebody else wants to cover. Free without a sponsor is just
[[ana-reyes]] paying for the venue, which is what January was.

## The cost it hides

⚠️ Free demand is untracked demand. 260 signups for 90 seats is not 260 people coming — ~43%
showed up, against the 60% Ana planned for. The pattern delivers a full room and takes away
the ability to know how full. [[dev-okonkwo]] named this on 15 April and it remains the
unresolved half of the trade: see [[filtering-versus-filling]].
