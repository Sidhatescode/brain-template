# Ana Reyes

Co-founder of [[lantern-club]] with [[dev-okonkwo]]. Runs the programming, the posters and
the sponsor relationship. Funded the first screenings out of her own flat budget.

## How she argues

She converts cost problems into product problems. Confronted with £68 of profit on nine
days of work, she does not propose raising the ticket price or cutting the venue — she
proposes removing the price entirely and selling the audience instead. The move rests on one
claim, stated flatly: the room is the product, and a hundred people who chose to spend a
Tuesday night on an unknown film are worth more to a brand than £8 a head.

She prices off perceived audience value, not cost recovery. The £400/night quote to
Northfield Reels has no stated basis — not a CPM, not a cost-plus figure. It was invented on
the call and accepted without negotiation, which suggests it was low.

## Decisiveness, and its cost

She takes certainty over optionality when offered both. Given the choice between booking one
sponsored night or three, she took three: "the certainty is worth more than the optionality
right now." That covered the venue through July off one conversation.

The same decisiveness produces her one recorded failure mode. The February inbound from
Northfield Reels — the thing the entire sponsorship model turned out to rest on — went
unanswered for a month and unmentioned to her co-founder, because she judged it unreal. Dev
learned about it mid-argument. ⚠️ Worth watching whether other inbound gets filtered by her
private assessment before Dev sees it; one instance is not a pattern.

## With Dev

She concedes ground she cannot defend rather than arguing through it. On the March call she
answers his objection about the room losing its character with "that's fair, I don't have an
answer to that," and proposes a bounded experiment instead. She also accepts his constraint
on the logo without pushing. The working relationship is not symmetric — she sets direction,
he bounds it — but it is not adversarial.
