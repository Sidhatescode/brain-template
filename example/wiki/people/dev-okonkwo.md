# Dev Okonkwo

Co-founder of [[lantern-club]] with [[ana-reyes]]. The project's sceptic, and the source of
every objection in the record that turned out to be worth writing down.

## Concede the direction, constrain the blast radius

His characteristic move is not to block. On the March pricing call he does not argue against
sponsorship; he names the trade — the thing that makes the night good versus the thing that
makes it survive — and then bounds the experiment: one night, April, and the logo nowhere
except the pre-roll. Not the poster, not the Luma page.

That bounding is why the April result is readable at all. A sponsored night with logos
everywhere would have confounded the pricing question with the character question. He
produced a clean test by negotiating the constraint, which is a more useful contribution
than being right would have been.

## He updates in writing, fast

Two reversals inside a month, both explicit. On 8 April, four hours after the free Luma page
took 140 signups: "the free thing is real then. deptford was the price." On 23 April, after
Priya's exit survey: "fine. i was wrong about the logo." Neither is hedged.

What he does not retract is the 15 April objection, and he separates it deliberately —
"not arguing against free. just noting we lost something." ⚠️ That distinction is the most
valuable thing in the April record and the easiest to lose, because the sponsorship result
was good enough to bury it. It is preserved in [[filtering-versus-filling]].

## The constraint nobody owns

He is the only person who treats process as the binding problem. "Nine days for forty-one
people is not a business, it's a hobby with admin" is the first time either founder names
operations as the limit, and he repeats it after the April success, when the result would
have excused dropping it. He asks for the runbook twice. He does not write it — Priya does.
