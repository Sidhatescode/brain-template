# Filtering versus filling

[[lantern-club]] can have a full room or a known room. It currently has a full one and has
stopped being able to describe it. The tension is live, it was named before the decision that
created it, and the good result has made it harder to see rather than easier.

## The two sides

**Filling.** Removing the door price took signups from 41 to 260 and attendance from 41 to
112. The sponsor pays more for that room than the audience ever did, the project went from
£68 a night to £200 a night net, and the renewal arrived unsolicited. On every number
anyone tracks, free is better. [[the-free-room-fills-itself]] is the mechanism.

**Filtering.** A price does work beyond revenue. It tells you who is coming. 41 people who
paid £8 are 41 people who will turn up; 260 free signups are an unknown fraction of a
number. [[dev-okonkwo]] put it plainly on 15 April — paying filters — and separated it from
the pricing argument so it would not be settled by the pricing result.

## Why it is not resolved

It looks resolved and isn't, for two reasons.

**The exit survey tested the weak version.** Priya asked 20 people whether the sponsor
bothered them; 18 hadn't noticed. But the logo had been deliberately confined to the
pre-roll, and the question was asked of people who came. It cannot reach the people who
didn't, and the strong version of the concern was never about the logo — it was about
whether a room anyone can walk into for free is the same room. Dev conceded the logo point,
correctly, and that concession has been allowed to stand in for the larger one.

**The first real cost has already landed and was read as a rounding error.** Ana planned the
April room against a 60% show-up rate. The actual was ~43%. At 260 signups that gap is the
difference between a comfortable 112 and 156 people at a 90-seat door. ⚠️ It worked out
because the number came in low, not because the planning was sound. The next sponsored night
with better conversion is the one that breaks.

## What would actually resolve it

Not another survey of attendees. Either:

- **A reservation mechanic that costs something other than money** — a confirmation step, a
  deposit refunded at the door, a waitlist that releases seats. These restore the filter
  without restoring the price, and nobody has tried one.
- **Or an explicit decision to stop caring**, written down: accept that attendance is
  unforecastable, book rooms against signups with a stated assumption, and stop treating
  overflow as a failure.

Doing neither is the current state, and it is a choice by default. Related: the nine-days-per
-night operations problem has the same shape — a real constraint that the April success made
easy to postpone.
