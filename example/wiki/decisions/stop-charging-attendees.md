# Stop charging attendees

Decided 2026-03-11 on a call between [[ana-reyes]] and [[dev-okonkwo]], executed 2026-04-02
when Northfield Reels agreed £400 for one night. [[lantern-club]] stopped selling tickets and
started selling the room.

## The reasoning, as stated

1. **The ticket wasn't paying for the thing anyway.** £328 gross on 41 tickets, less £200
   venue and £60 for a projector bulb, is £68 for nine days of work. The price was not
   funding the project; it was decorating a loss.
2. **The price was suppressing the audience.** 210 Luma views, 41 conversions. Ana attributed
   the whole 20% to the £8. The January Hackney night — free, 94 people in a 60-seat room —
   was the counter-case.
3. **The audience is worth more wholesale than retail.** A full room of people who chose an
   obscure film on a weeknight is a thing a brand will pay to stand in front of, and it
   prices above £8 a head.
4. **The demand already existed, inbound.** Northfield Reels had written in February asking
   what a pre-film logo cost. The decision did not require finding a sponsor, only answering
   one.

## What was conceded to get it

Dev did not oppose the direction. He named the trade — the character of the room against the
survival of the project — and bought two constraints in exchange for agreeing:

- **One night, not a policy.** April, with an explicit question asked of attendees afterwards.
- **Pre-roll only.** No sponsor logo on the poster or the Luma page.

Both constraints held, and they are the reason the April result means anything. A fully
branded night would have confounded the pricing question with the character question.

## What happened

Free Luma page: 140 signups in four hours, 260 in two weeks against a 90-seat room. 112
attended. Priya's exit survey of 20 people found 18 had not noticed the sponsor and 2 did
not care. Dev withdrew the logo objection in writing. Northfield asked about three more
nights during the screening itself; £1,200 was agreed by 27 April, covering the venue through
July. Ana took all three nights rather than one at a time, on the grounds that certainty beat
optionality.

## What it did not settle

⚠️ The decision was validated on revenue and attendance, which were never Dev's objection.
His 15 April point — that 260 untrackable signups is a worse planning position than 41 people
who paid, because paying filters — survives the result intact, and he flagged it as separate
on purpose. The ~43% show-up rate against Ana's assumed 60% is the first cost landing. See
[[filtering-versus-filling]].

The £400 also went unexamined. It was invented on the March call with no stated basis and
accepted without negotiation, which usually means it was under market. Renewal gave them
leverage they did not have and did not use.
